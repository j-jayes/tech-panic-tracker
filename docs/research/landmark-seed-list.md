## Apocryphal / misquotation hazards (codebook warning list)

1. **Warren Bennis "factory of the future… a man and a dog": APOCRYPHAL** — anonymous joke in *Datamation* (1978); Bennis disclaimed authorship (https://quoteinvestigator.com/2022/01/30/future-factory/). Never seed as a Bennis prediction.
2. **Reuther/Ford "how will you get robots to buy cars?": folklore** — genericized story from the mid-1950s, no documented exchange (https://quoteinvestigator.com/2011/11/16/robots-buy-cars/).
3. **Keynes 1930** — genuine, but explicitly "a temporary phase of maladjustment"; the 15-hour week is a living-standards forecast, not job-loss.
4. **Simon "within twenty years"** — genuine but 1960, not 1965; date accordingly.
5. **Leontief horses** — genuine in substance; the one-liner truncates the 1983 passage; quote in full.
6. **Mill 1848** — genuine (QI-verified) but retrospective, not predictive.
7. **McKinsey "800 million jobs lost"** — media shorthand; evaluable figures are 75–375m switchers, 400–800m only in the rapid-adoption scenario (→ `estimate_type = range` + `magnitude_headline`).
